Why a value changed

Every change comes from one of five corrections. Each row in the list names the one that applies.

  • Stat order117 items · all up

    Two items with the same stats could score differently depending on the order the stats were stored in. Order no longer matters.

  • Split stats13 items · 9 up, 4 down

    A stat listed twice, such as STR +3 and STR +3, scored lower than a single STR +6. The total is now valued once.

  • Spell procs13 items · all up

    Items that fire one of several spells at random lost a point or more to early rounding. The fraction is now kept until the end.

  • Zero stats4 items · 1 up, 3 down

    A stat slot holding +0 shifted the value even though it grants nothing. It now counts for nothing.

  • Inactive proc1 item · down

    The claymore of Zarthos was credited for a spell it cannot cast. With that credit removed it drops from 338 to 111, and can be forged from level 37.

Randomly generated gear

Random drops have no fixed entry to list, so we tested 8,720 generated samples instead. About 6% of them changed, split evenly: 269 went up and 258 went down, typically by around 10 points.

A random item is affected only if it mixes flat and percentage stats, repeats a stat, or carries a +0 stat. Lore or identify it to see its current value.

What follows the number

Item value sets the level needed to craft or enhance an item, the materials a recipe uses, the grade of salvage, and which faerie bag an item can drop from. For most of this list a few points changes none of that. Where a value crosses a threshold, the requirement moves with it: for 64 items the level needed to craft or enhance them shifts, 52 of them by a single level.

Enhancing follows the same rule as crafting and forging: the item you enhance must be within your level's limit of three item value per level, and each enhancement raises it by 1 to 4. The old fixed ceiling on enhanced items is gone, and 33 new items fill gaps where a piece of gear had no next step.

Figures compare the same items before and after the update. Items you have already enhanced are valued from their own stats, by the same corrected rules.

Why we made these changes

Item value decides what you can craft, forge, enhance and salvage. A number with that much say over your gear has to be one you can trust.

  • Same stats, same value

    Two identical items could score differently because of how their stats happened to be stored. Now equal gear is always worth the same.

  • Credit only for what works

    An item is valued for what it actually does. A spell it cannot cast no longer inflates its value, and an empty +0 no longer shifts it.

  • One number everywhere

    Lore, crafting, forging, enhancing, salvage and quest rewards all read the same calculation, so the value you see is the value the game uses.

  • Your gear is untouched

    No stat was changed and nothing was rebalanced. Only the way stats are counted was corrected, and {{UP}} of the {{COUNT}} changes went up.

  • Nothing lost on a failure

    A craft, forge or enhancement that is refused or cannot finish leaves your gear, materials and coins as they were. The price shown is the price charged.

  • Checked against the whole world

    Every one of the 19,367 items was valued before and after, and tested gear kept its value through rent, lockers, death and restarts. This page is the complete result.
